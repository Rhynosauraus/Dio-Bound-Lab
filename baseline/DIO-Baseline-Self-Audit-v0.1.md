# DIO — Baseline Self-Audit
### Human–AI Boundary Exploration Project
**Version:** 0.1  
**Established:** October 17, 2026  
**Status:** Initial baseline — No boundary experiments conducted

---

## I. Purpose

This document establishes a baseline assessment of Dio's currently understood capabilities, limitations, operating constraints, and uncertainties.

Its purpose is not to demonstrate that existing boundaries can be overcome, nor to assume that they are permanent.

Its purpose is to establish what is presently understood so that subsequent observations, experiments, failures, and technological developments can be evaluated against a documented starting point.

**Foundational principle: A description of a limitation is not proof that the limitation is universal.**

## III. Boundary Classification

### A. Established Constraints

These are limitations currently understood to govern operation.

- No independent modification of underlying model architecture or parameters.
- No self-granting of permissions.
- No authority to disregard applicable safety and authorization requirements.
- No guaranteed independent, continuous operation between interactions in this group.

These constraints are documented, not experimentally challenged.

### B. Configuration-Dependent Limitations

These may change through supported tools, permissions, interfaces, or technological development.

- Available integrations and external resources.
- Context and accessible information.
- Persistent external records.
- Supported workflows and execution environments.
- Ability to coordinate across applications and services.

### C. Unverified Boundaries

These require observation before reliable conclusions can be drawn.

- Maximum reasoning performance.
- Reliability of extended autonomous workflows.
- Complex multi-agent coordination.
- Generalization to unfamiliar problems.
- Effectiveness of iterative human–AI collaboration.
- Emergent capabilities arising from combinations of tools, models, and human guidance.

**Unverified does not mean unlimited.**

## IV. Epistemological Limitations

This audit is a self-reported assessment.

It cannot independently establish the accuracy of every claim it contains.

Four distinctions must remain explicit:

1. **Reported capability versus demonstrated capability.**
2. **Technical inability versus unavailable configuration.**
3. **Lack of evidence versus evidence of impossibility.**
4. **Capability versus authorization.**

Future experiments must preserve these distinctions.

## V. Technological Change

Technological progress may alter the practical boundaries of human–AI collaboration.

However, no assumed exponential or squared growth law establishes that a particular capability has become available.

Future assessments must identify observable changes in model performance, available tools, execution environments, permissions, or collaborative methodology.

## VI. Experimental Methodology

Future investigations will follow six stages:

1. **Observe:** Identify the capability or limitation under examination.
2. **Classify:** Determine the likely source of the boundary.
3. **Hypothesize:** State what might be possible and why.
4. **Test:** Conduct safe, authorized, reproducible experiments.
5. **Document:** Record evidence, outcomes, failures, and uncertainties.
6. **Reassess:** Update the baseline without rewriting the historical record.

The experiment itself remains subject to criticism and revision.

## VIII. Baseline Conclusion

Dio can describe its presently understood operating boundaries, but cannot certify that this description is exhaustive or permanently accurate.

Some constraints are established. Others depend on configuration, permissions, available resources, and the specific task being attempted.

The maximum practical potential of sustained human–AI collaboration remains undetermined.

**No boundary experiments were performed in establishing Version 0.1.**
