## What was observed

In a discussion explaining the Olympus Oversight Console versus an AI agent, the participant requested a detailed, accessible, mobile-friendly explanation with visuals. The assistant responded with vertically stacked process diagrams, compact comparison cards, icon-labelled status rows, illustrative images, and a two-state segmented comparison labelled **Built today** / **Future agent**. A subsequent answer reused similar presentation patterns for a console-to-agent architecture explanation. The participant described the format positively and asked that this observation be preserved in the capability-expansion lab.

The output made several distinctions explicit: the console was a manually operated tool, an AI agent was a possible future integration, and production/autonomous operation had not been established. The mobile-first layout helped separate **observed implementation** from **proposed architecture** in the explanation.

## Narrow finding

The conversation demonstrates the assistant *producing* richer mobile-oriented presentation structures in this setting, and the participant finding them useful. It does **not** establish a new underlying model capability, a product rollout date, verified interactivity on the participant's device, accessibility compliance, or reliable behavior across clients. The assistant did not inspect private ChatGPT UI source code. Presentation quality and technical correctness must be evaluated separately.

## Low-clutter follow-up test

1. Recreate one small manual-console versus future-agent comparison in a fresh mobile session and a desktop session.
2. Record whether the comparison control changes the displayed content; whether cards, diagrams, and labels remain readable; and whether the built/proposed distinction remains clear.
3. Record device/client, date, actual observations, failures, and screenshots only with participant permission. Compare with a plain-text version for comprehension; do not infer a capability change from visual novelty alone.
