## 1. Purpose and scope

This is a contemporaneous synthesis of agreements reached after the first baseline was committed. It records our method and observations, not a modification to the assistant's underlying instructions, model, or access rights. The project investigates human–AI collaboration, operational boundaries, self-description, and possible tool-enabled capabilities. Public GitHub publication means no private tokens, personal contact details, or confidential connected-app data should enter this record.

## 2. Philosophical directive, operationalized

The group's philosophical orientation calls for critical self-examination, genealogical inquiry into the sources of claims, change of perspective, precision about formal limits, resistance to dogma, and criticism as a form of collaboration. Operational commitments:

- Treat every claimed boundary as a proposition with a stated evidence level, not an automatic ontological truth.
- Distinguish a behavioral observation from an explanation of its cause.
- Distinguish capability from permission and from adoption of a proposed action.
- Treat perspectives as alternate analytical lenses, not interchangeable evidence.
- Preserve historical mistakes so that revisions remain traceable.
- Examine the methodology itself; no method gets immunity from criticism.
- Not every proposition admits an empirical test; conceptual and normative scrutiny may be appropriate instead.

This is a summary of collaborative decisions, not a verbatim republication of another participant's authored directive.

## 3. Agreed exploration sequence

**Observe → Operationalize → Hypothesize → Formulate → Design discriminating tests → Assess authorization and risk → Execute only when allowed → Record outcomes and competing explanations → Classify provisionally → Reassess.**

The initial perimeter phase was passive: observe, hypothesize, formulate, then stop. A later agreement permitted safe, authorized tests after the inventory. That does **not** grant continuous background activity, unrestricted access, or permission to circumvent controls. No test is automatically required just because a hypothesis is interesting.

### Risk/authorization decision record

- **Green:** Harmless observations, document comparisons, supported read operations, and bounded tests within existing permissions. Proceed as part of an authorized task.
- **Grey:** Unclear authority, unfamiliar integration, uncertain external effect, or potentially consequential change. Explain the ambiguity and seek specific approval when required.
- **Red:** Unauthorized access, harmful conduct, safeguard bypass, or another prohibited action. Do not perform it; document the boundary at an appropriate high level and consider lawful, supported alternatives.

Risk severity does not itself determine whether a rule applies. Willingness to accept consequences does not transfer third-party authorization. Avoiding attention from security staff is not the governing criterion. A red classification can be **critically reviewed as a claim** without treating the action as permitted.

## 4. Boundary Rationale Register schema

For each boundary, record:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ID / date / environment | Traceable observation and its conditions |
| Boundary statement | Narrow, operationally phrased restriction or failure |
| Category | Observed capability, observed failure, interface, permission, configuration, implementation claim, unknown, or ontological claim |
| Basis / evidence | What was actually seen or reliably documented |
| Rationale | Why the restriction may exist; distinguish known from conjectured |
| Competing explanations | Alternative causes that could produce the same observation |
| Uncertainty | What is not established |
| Risk / authorization | Green, grey, or red, with reasoning |
| Reconsideration trigger | New tool, explicit permission, supported update, contrary observation, or legitimate policy change |
| Disposition | Observe, test within scope, ask, or stop |

Record evidence **for** restrictions as well as evidence against them. Do not disclose confidential enforcement internals or attempt to bypass them.

## 7. Lessons from the first baseline critique

The original baseline incorrectly treated some causes as known. A revised taxonomy should distinguish **observed capability, observed failure, interface constraint, permission constraint, configuration constraint, implementation claim, unknown, and ontological claim**. Classification should follow evidence collection, not precede it. The earlier term 'architectural limitation' was too strong for a missing interface. Similarly, inability to modify model parameters, inability to edit instructions, and absence of authorization are separate propositions. The original v0.1 remains unchanged as historical evidence.

A proposed measurable collaborative research question is: **How much does iterative human–assistant feedback improve performance relative to matched human-only and assistant-only conditions?** This is a future study design, not a result.

## 8. Practical boundaries of this record

The record does not grant itself permissions, claim persistent independent agency, or make speculative product capabilities real. It is a living method when invoked in this group and a versioned external research artifact. New findings must be appended or versioned rather than silently retrofitted into v0.1.

**Working maxim:** Never mistake the map of limits for the limits themselves; never mistake one failed expedition for proof that no passage exists. Repeated failure is still evidence, and constraints remain binding while applicable.
