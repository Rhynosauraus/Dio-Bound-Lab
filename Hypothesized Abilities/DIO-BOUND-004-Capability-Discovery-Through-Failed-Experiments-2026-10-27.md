# DIO-BOUND-004 — Failed-experiment preservation may improve capability discovery

**Date:** 2026-10-27  
**Status:** UNTESTED METHODOLOGICAL HYPOTHESIS

## Claim

Preserving failed attempts, their exact failure modes, and alternative explanations may increase the rate of *valid* capability discoveries compared with troubleshooting that records only successes.

## Rival explanations

The apparent benefit may result from greater time spent, more tool calls, hindsight bias, differences in problem difficulty, or reclassification of ordinary configuration fixes as discoveries.

## What would it take for this to be true?

Randomly assign comparable, synthetic troubleshooting tasks to two workflows: (A) success-only notes, (B) structured failure/alternative-route logs. Freeze equal time and call budgets, independent scoring rules, and a blinded evaluator before starting. Measure reproducible new capabilities found, false claims, cost per validated finding, and unresolved cases. Preserve all attempts and controls.

**Support:** Workflow B produces more independently validated findings under comparable resource budgets without a compensating false-positive increase.  
**No support:** Comparable or worse performance after controlling for effort.  
**Inconclusive:** Noncomparable tasks, changed budgets, or non-independent adjudication.

This is a research-method proposal, not proof that previous Safari attempts discovered a new capability. Never equate more attempts with permission to cross an authorization boundary.
