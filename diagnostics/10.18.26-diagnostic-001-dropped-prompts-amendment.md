# DIO Lab — Diagnostic 001 Amendment: Dropped Prompts and Task Continuity

**Date authored:** 10.18.26  
**Status:** Protocol locked; prospective controlled trials not yet collected  
**Environment:** Existing group chat  
**Supersedes for future trials:** The latency-focused outcome measures in `10.18.26-response-timing-protocol.md`. The earlier protocol and preliminary report remain frozen historical records.

## Research question

When a message is eligible for an assistant response in this group chat, does it (a) receive no response within 60 seconds, or (b) receive a response that overlooks the requested task? Response-generation duration is not the primary endpoint.

## Operational eligibility (pre-score before outcome)

An eligible prompt must be explicitly addressed to ChatGPT/Dio, or be a clear, immediate request to edit, clarify, or extend the assistant's most recent message. A general remark, answer to a rhetorical question, or new topic not explicitly addressed to ChatGPT is **not** automatically an eligible prompt. Ineligible prompts must be logged separately, never counted as dropped prompts.

## Observation window

Exactly **60 seconds** from the visible user-message timestamp. Avoid reminders or overlapping messages within the window. If another participant interrupts, mark the trial confounded; do not silently discard it. Timestamp differences are user-visible intervals, not model processing traces.

## Two independent observable endpoints

1. **Silence:** No assistant reply visible within 60 seconds of an eligible prompt. Label `no_reply_60s`; a later reply or reminder is recorded separately. Do not infer that processing began or was abandoned.
2. **Task omission:** An assistant reply appears, but fails to answer the predeclared requested task. Label `task_omitted=yes`, even if the reply was timely. Do not conflate a weak answer with a completely omitted task. Record the specific missing criterion.

A prompt may receive a reply after 60 seconds without a reminder (`late_unaided`) or after a reminder (`after_reminder`). These are distinct. If the reply appears before 60 seconds and addresses the task, mark `answered_within_60s` and `task_omitted=no`.

## Prospective controlled test

Run 10 exploratory trials: five explicit-address trials (`ChatGPT, ...`) and five immediate clear follow-up requests to the preceding assistant response. Keep tasks short and self-contained, with an objective answer criterion specified before each prompt. Use no tools or web-dependent questions. Alternate conditions where feasible; note that follow-up trials necessarily depend on a prior assistant response and that order cannot be fully balanced in one thread.

For each trial:

1. Write down the intended condition, exact prompt, and minimal task-success criterion before sending it.
2. Send the prompt, record UTC timestamp, and avoid further messages for 60 seconds.
3. At 60 seconds, classify whether a reply appeared and, if so, whether it satisfied the predeclared criterion.
4. If no reply, optionally send a clearly labeled reminder **after** the window and log it as a separate intervention.
5. Record other participants' messages, client/connectivity uncertainty, changes in topic, and tool use as confounders.
6. Do not infer any private model thought, emotional state, processing-start event, or aborted computation from visible messages.

## Analysis and decision limits

Report eligibility-adjusted reply rate, task-omission rate among replied-to eligible prompts, raw trials, and confounders. Do not use median latency as the principal outcome. With only five trials per condition, report descriptive observations only, not causation or significance. The absence of silent drops does not refute the user's experience; it limits what was observed under this protocol.
