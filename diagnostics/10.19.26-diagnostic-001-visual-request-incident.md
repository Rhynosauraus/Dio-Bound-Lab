## Eligibility analysis under locked protocol

- The initial visual request **did not explicitly address ChatGPT/Dio**. It introduced a new task after a discussion of Russian novelists rather than clearly editing or clarifying the assistant's immediately preceding answer. Under the predeclared group-chat eligibility rule, it is **not a confirmed eligible prompt** and must **not** be scored as a `no_reply_60s` controlled failure.
- The first “chat” is an explicit address, but contains **no standalone task or preregistered success criterion**. No reply appeared within 60 seconds of that ping. Record this as an **unanswered addressed ping**, not as a controlled task-omission trial.
- The later “CHAT!!!! Wtf mate” is also an explicit address without a new task criterion. The assistant replied within 60 seconds, using earlier context to supply the requested visual.
- No controlled 60-second observation window was preregistered for this sequence. It should remain separate from the prospective trial CSV.

## Interpretation and design implications

The user experienced a real, extended delay in getting an explicitly requested visual. The eligibility rule explains why the first message cannot be counted as a *confirmed protocol-defined dropped prompt*, but it does **not** erase the user-experience failure. A human-readable request may fail a system-specific addressing gate; those are distinct measurements.

The record does **not** identify whether the cause was message routing, group-chat addressing rules, client delivery, model generation, or something else. Avoid attributing private model state.

## Proposed follow-up

1. Continue the locked D001 ten-trial series without changing eligibility criteria midstream.
2. Separately design a future observational/controlled UX study comparing explicitly addressed new tasks with unaddressed new tasks in group chat. Predeclare a user-intent measure and a routing-eligibility measure as **different variables**.
3. Preserve this case as an incident, not an additional failed controlled trial.
