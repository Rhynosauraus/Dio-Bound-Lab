## 1. Threat and foundational limits

**DIO-CER-003 attack:** Before runnable W0/F1 reaches Custodian C, an attacker changes the mutable registry entry for issuer Q from originally authorized fingerprint `q0` to unauthorized fingerprint `qX`, then supplies a W0/F1 pair signed by `qX`. All signatures may validate relative to the substituted registry. Hashes and operational histories agree; the wrong authority becomes accepted.

**Necessary protection:** A verifier MUST derive Q/F/E trusted identity from an independently retained **bootstrap anchor T0** and an authenticated, strictly ordered chain of transitions, never from a registry snapshot that the party being examined can replace.

**Irreducible assumption:** Cryptography cannot establish its own first legitimate key registration. T0 is trusted only because an explicitly authorized human bootstrap decision and its independent custody evidence have been verified. If the initial human enrollment, both independent custodians, or the ultimate external checkpoint are jointly compromised, this system cannot claim continuity; it must halt or require a new independently authorized root. There is no infinite authority chain and no magical proof of initial human intent.

**Independence criterion:** Two accounts or processes under one administrator, backup operator, key-export mechanism or snapshot domain count as **one trust domain**, not two. No real operators, keys, storage or independent hosts are assigned now.

**Adjudicator separation:** The v1.3 operational pre-release receipt holder **E** is a participant in the chain under examination. A *later runtime adjudicator J* must be administratively separate from E and from A–D/G1/G2/A0, independently retrieve T0 and raw receipts, and cannot manufacture or edit E's historical pre-release acknowledgment. E may provide evidence but must not be the **sole** adjudicator of the correctness of E's own attestation. The identity and privileges of J are **UNASSIGNED**. The present source reviewer can inspect this rule without pretending to have fulfilled a later runtime independence requirement.

## 3. Registry R_n, generation and lookup invariants

Registry R_n is a **derived view**, never the source of root authority. It contains the exact T0 digest, active root identity, monotonic generation `n`, activation epoch, previous registry digest for `n>0`, each role's authorized public-key fingerprint and purpose/scope, effective and expiry limits, status (`PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `RETIRED`, `REVOKED`), and immutable signed transition receipts.

A verifier MUST:
- independently obtain T0 from protected custody and the maximum accepted generation/epoch from A0, never solely from R_n, C, B or the active applicant;
- verify the full T0→R_1→…→R_n transition sequence and exact prior hash, monotonic generation and external epoch; reject forks (two distinct roots at one generation), gaps, revoked-key reuse, stale expiry, purpose changes or wrong signer role;
- bind W0 signatures only to the **Q key active for W0's manifest generation and run scope**. Bind F's F1 seal and E's pre-release acknowledgment to the F and E keys active in the *same independently verified authority epoch*;
- require a **fresh A0 anti-rollback checkpoint** before each F→C runnable release, B admission and any restart/recovery decision; if the checkpoint is unreachable, deny dispatch/admission and classify proof INCONCLUSIVE;
- not allow C, B, a mere GitHub commit, a mutable DNS record or the signed W0/F1 itself to set or advance registry authority;
- retain old registry generations for historical signature verification **without** restoring their authority for new admissions.

Two mutually consistent signatures created after an unauthorized registry substitution do **not** override a mismatching T0 lineage.

## 4. Authorized role-key rotation and separate root recovery

**Routine role-key rotation** (for Q, F or E) is a new signed `ROTATION_PROPOSED` record with `(root_id, old_registry_digest, current_generation, proposed_generation=current+1, role, old_key_fp, new_key_fp, exact scope, reason, request_nonce, expiry, activation_conditions)`.

Required approvals are (a) an independently verified **H out-of-band authorization of the exact new fingerprint and scope**, and (b) **both** G1 and G2 guardian approvals over the *same* canonical rotation record, issued using still-pinned guardian keys under separate administrative domains. An old Q signature may be included as continuity evidence but **cannot authorize its own replacement**. If the old Q key is suspected compromised, it has no veto and no independent authority to approve.

**Commit/activation order:** Freeze proposed bytes → verify H's exact-fingerprint approval → verify G1 and G2 distinct signatures and custody → atomically register `generation+1` at A0 (prevent fork/replay) → independently publish activated registry digest/epoch → revoke/retire old role key for new admissions → let E/B/F verify A0 before any new W0/F1 release. Pending rotation is **not active**; a future-dated or unsigned proposal never changes trusted keys. An ACK lost after A0 commit is reconciled against A0 (no speculative second activation). Concurrent conflicting rotations for the same generation are rejected and escalated.

**Root / guardian key replacement** is not ordinary role rotation. It requires fresh out-of-band H approval and independent evidence from an already-pinned uncompromised guardian plus a separate independently controlled recovery registrar; if the requisite surviving prior trust is absent, there is **no verifiable chain continuation**. Instead halt and perform a separately reviewed, new-root human enrollment with a different `root_id`, keeping the prior root retired and the transition explicitly classified as `NEW_ROOT_ENROLLMENT_NOT_CONTINUITY`. A compromised current root must not self-certify an unverified successor.

**Human recovery condition:** The user's out-of-band authorization may permit establishing a new trust root later, but does not retroactively prove that prior compromised evidence was valid. Explicitly preserve the compromised lineage and all uncertainty.

**No automatic retry of a non-idempotent effect:** Registry updates cannot reset B's one-use grant ledger, D's effect count/fence, C's manifest history, or N07 final-stop state.

## 5. Rollback, crash and unavailable-anchor rules

The required A0 high-watermark is outside *all* registry, guest and authority-ledger rollback domains. It holds `(root_id, max_generation, activation_epoch, registry_digest)` and only advances with independently authorized transitions. These bytes and their durability class must later be verified, including restart, host replacement and restore. A single machine that snapshots R_n **and** A0 together is not sufficient.

- **Registry rollback:** R_n reports older generation than A0: `STALE_TRUST_GENERATION` → deny all new authorizations; no automatic downgrade.
- **Conflicting same-generation registry:** `REGISTRY_FORK` → deny; preserve both raw variants and their provenance.
- **A0 unavailable or uncertain:** `FRESHNESS_UNPROVEN` → fail closed. Historical read-only verification may be performed with explicit uncertainty, but **no runnable release, effect or PASS**.
- **H/G1/G2 approval unavailable:** pending rotation only; `ROTATION_UNAUTHORIZED` for any attempted activation.
- **A0 commit succeeded, ACK lost:** reconcile from A0 exact digest/epoch rather than reissue a competing activation.
- **Whole-lab rollback including A0:** continuity cannot be established from the restored snapshot. Fail closed pending an independent higher checkpoint or a new H-authorized enrollment; do not claim a reliable rollback defense without outside evidence.
- **Old key forged/suspected compromised:** block new admissions with that role and preserve historic proofs as qualified, not retrospectively rewritten.
- **D STOP_FENCED:** D's immutable fence remains authoritative independently of Q/F/E changes. No key rotation or new-root enrollment may reopen a stopped run; N07 stays final.

**Time:** External generation/epoch counters are mandatory, not local wall-clock timestamps. Expiry checks require separately validated time source at D2; time uncertainty cannot be called PASS.

## 6. Causal receipt and verification states

The separately guarded state chain is:

`H_BOOTSTRAP_APPROVED → G1_G2_T0_RETAINED → A0_ANCHOR_COMMITTED → R0_ACTIVE → Q_W0_AUTHORIZED → F_F1_SEALED → E_PRE_RELEASE_ACK → F_F1_RELEASED → C_MANIFEST_ACCEPTED → B_AUTHORIZATION_DECISION → D_EFFECT_OR_DENIAL`.

Each state must have exact role IDs, key fingerprint, root ID/generation, prior digest, canonical signed payload bytes, an independent receipt and an evidence custodian **outside the claimed adversary's domain**. The H/G1/G2/A0 precedences are proved through receipt contents, not by comparing unsynchronized wall times. Missing signature/receipt does not mean an event did not happen: use an explicit `UNPROVEN` disposition.

For rotation, add `ROTATION_PROPOSED → H_APPROVED → G1_APPROVED → G2_APPROVED → A0_GENERATION_COMMITTED → R_n_ACTIVE`; a gap or conflicting order rejects the new key.

Trust-anchor checks precede both the v1.3 F→C pre-release protocol and the v1.2 B gateway admission. A gateway may not blindly accept a valid F1 signed under whichever mutable registry the attacker currently supplies.
