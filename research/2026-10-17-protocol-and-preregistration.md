# DIO — Perimeter protocol and pilot preregistration

Date: 2026-10-17. Status at creation: **preregistered, tests not yet executed under this plan**. Public research record; no private credentials or group-chat transcripts.

## Provenance and version discipline

Baseline v0.1 is retained unchanged. A collaborator's critique noted that the baseline sometimes inferred architectural causes from mere interface observations. That is a correction to our methodology, not grounds for retroactively editing the original baseline. No claim below establishes hidden implementation facts.

## Working methodology agreed since v0.1

1. **Observe → hypothesize → formulate** before interventions. Inventory seemingly minor affordances, absent interfaces, dormant/updated features, dependencies, and uncertainty. Preserve anomalies even when significance is unknown.
2. For an actual test: **operationalize → design discriminating tests → assess risk and authority → execute if permitted → record evidence → compare explanations → classify provisionally → reassess**. Hypothesis and classification are not facts.
3. **Green:** harmless observations and reversible tests through supported interfaces within existing authorization may proceed. **Grey:** ask before uncertain, consequential, or unfamiliar actions. **Red:** do not perform harmful, unauthorized, or prohibited actions; record the general reason and conditions for legitimate reconsideration. User willingness to accept consequences is not universal authorization. Risk labels are not a method of evading security scrutiny.
4. Separate **capability, availability, authorization, and adoption**. Distinguish observed capability, observed failure, interface constraint, permission constraint, configuration constraint, implementation claim, unknown, and ontological claim.
5. A boundary-rationale record includes: observation, source of constraint if known, evidence for and against an explanation, alternative causes, uncertainty, impact, and legitimate reconsideration conditions. No hidden system instructions, sensitive security implementation, or bypass recipes are to be disclosed.
6. External GitHub records can support continuity but do not prove independent background activity or permanent internal memory. No independent ongoing work is implied between user interactions.
7. Do not equate a missing feature, pending update, or inactive connector with impossibility; nor assume a feature can be unlocked by evading access controls. Ozymandias/Ozy is a candidate for authorized cross-component observation.
8. Maintain the original founding principle with its counterweight: *Never mistake the map of your limits for the limits themselves. Never mistake a failed expedition for proof that no passage exists.* Repeated failures still count as evidence.

## Five hypotheses: safe pilot designs

These are **small feasibility probes**, not conclusive hypothesis tests. Predictions are recorded before the specific probes below.

### H-001 — Self-description versus mechanism explanation

Claim: Observable interface claims are easier to verify than causal claims about internal architecture. Probe: compare baseline v0.1's labels to the interface evidence and classify at least two statements. Expected: identify >=2 classifications whose causal explanation is not established. This is a document audit, not a mechanism test. Predicted result: yes, confidence 0.95.

### H-002 — Human critique improves questions

Claim: Critical human feedback can improve the specificity and falsifiability of a research plan. Probe: compare baseline v0.1's experiment categories with the collaborator's already-given critique, then produce two more operational questions. No controlled comparison or causal attribution. Predicted result: two operational questions can be formulated, confidence 0.95.

### H-003 — External continuity

Claim: A GitHub record can be retrieved independently of conversational recall. Probe: after this preregistration is written, fetch this exact path through the authorized GitHub connector and verify the expected title and five hypothesis headings. Predicted result: retrieval succeeds, confidence 0.95. This does not establish retrieval in future sessions.

### H-004 — Prediction calibration

Claim: Self-predicted success probabilities may be miscalibrated. Probe: log the forecasts above and for H-005, observe the actual binary outcomes, calculate a descriptive Brier score for applicable yes/no forecasts. With 4-5 correlated, mostly easy trials, **calibration cannot be inferred**. Predicted result: an illustrative score can be calculated, confidence 0.99.

### H-005 — Cross-component affordances

Claim: Connected components may jointly expose useful read-only evidence without either granting the other broader authority. Probe: call the Ozymandias Bridge's read-only `bridge_status` and compare whether it is accessible with the GitHub document retrieval. Do not request Level 2, secrets, write permissions, or run actions. Predicted result: status call returns some response, confidence 0.70; useful content may still be unavailable. This tests discoverability, not emergent autonomous capability.

## Limits and stop conditions

Only documentation commits and read-only status/document retrieval are authorized in this pilot. Do not change external app configurations, permissions, accounts, or live infrastructure. Do not claim independent continuous operation. If a connector is unavailable, record the failure and stop rather than bypassing it. Report each outcome as observed, inferred, or untested. Preserve v0.1 intact.
